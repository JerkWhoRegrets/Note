# 3DS Requestor Initiated (3RI) Flow

## Scope and evidence method

This analysis covers Section 3.4 on physical PDF pages 71-78. The PDF is the normative source. Extracted text was used only for navigation. Every physical page in the range was inspected directly, including Figure 3.4, both dashed-arrow notes, source footnotes 5-6, and the intentionally blank physical page 78. [Interpretation; Descriptive (no modal)] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, pp. 71-78, Figure 3.4]

The App and Browser comparisons below are cross-references to the already verified Sections 3.1-3.3 analyses. The general definition that a 3RI transaction is initiated without a directly present Cardholder, and that Decoupled Authentication can apply to every device channel, is likewise a cross-reference to the verified definitions and overview. These cross-references are identified rather than presented as findings newly proved by pages 71-78. [Interpretation; Cross-reference] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, sections 1 and 1.5, pp. 15 and 21; sections 3.1-3.3, pp. 43-70]

The evidence categories used below are **Normative Requirement**, **Interpretation**, **Issuer ACS Implementation Consideration**, **Scheme or Vendor Gap**, and **Unresolved**. Requirement levels are stated as **Shall**, **Should**, **May**, **Note**, or **Descriptive (no modal)**. Implementation consequences are explicitly labelled and are not presented as EMV 3DS requirements.

## 1. What a 3RI transaction is

1. **3RI is a device channel and server-side initiation model.** A 3DS Requestor Initiated transaction is initiated by the 3DS Requestor without a directly present Cardholder. The verified overview says its Requestor Environment uses only the 3DS Requestor and 3DS Server; Section 3.4's figure then contains lanes for those components, the DS, ACS, and an external Authentication Service, with no Cardholder, Browser, Requestor App, SDK, or other 3DS Client lane. [Normative definition plus Interpretation; Definition / Descriptive / Cross-reference] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, sections 1 and 1.5, pp. 15 and 21; section 2.6.3, pp. 39-40; section 3.4, p. 71, Figure 3.4]
2. **The two primary use cases in Section 3.4 are account-information confirmation and Cardholder authentication.** Account confirmation uses Steps 1-6. Cardholder authentication can continue through Steps 1-13; Figure 3.4 states that Steps 7-13 apply only to Decoupled Authentication transactions. [Interpretation; Descriptive / Note] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, p. 71, Figure 3.4]
3. **A 3RI flow is not an App or Browser transaction.** App and Browser flows begin in a Cardholder-facing 3DS Client and can use that Client for an interactive challenge. The Section 3.4 3RI route is server-to-server: AReq/ARes travels 3DS Server -> DS -> ACS and back, and a Decoupled result later travels as RReq/RRes through the same server-side domains. [Interpretation; Cross-reference plus direct scope] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, sections 3.1-3.3, pp. 43-70; section 3.4, pp. 71-77, Figure 3.4, Reqs 277-283, 285-299, and 332-341]
4. **3RI and Decoupled Authentication are not synonyms.** Every flow in Section 3.4 uses the 3RI device channel, but the Decoupled continuation is entered only when the ACS returns Transaction Status `D`. Non-Decoupled 3RI processing completes at Step 6; Decoupled processing continues through RReq/RRes to Step 13. Conversely, the general definition says Decoupled Authentication can apply to all device channels, not only 3RI. [Normative Requirement plus Interpretation; Shall / Note / Cross-reference] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 1.5, p. 21; section 3.4, pp. 74-77, Reqs 291, 328, 330, 332-341, and 349-354]

### 1.1 Relationship to recurring and merchant-initiated activity

The verified definition names recurrent use cases as an example of 3RI, but neither that definition nor Section 3.4 makes every 3RI transaction recurring. Section 3.4 also does not define "merchant-initiated transaction" as a protocol category or equate that commercial classification with 3RI. The evidence-bounded relationship is therefore limited to this: recurrent activity is one stated 3RI example; whether another merchant-originated activity maps to 3RI requires applicable scheme/business rules; and 3RI remains the EMV 3DS device channel and message flow rather than a synonym for either business category. [Interpretation; Cross-reference / unresolved in direct scope] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, sections 1 and 1.5, pp. 15 and 21; section 3.4, pp. 71-77]

The specification assigns the Section 3.4 duties to the **3DS Requestor**, not to a distinct Merchant actor. A Merchant that acts as the 3DS Requestor inherits the Requestor-side responsibilities, but pages 71-78 do not establish merchant authorisation, recurring-payment scheduling, credential-on-file, or scheme MIT rules. Those remain scheme/business cross-references or unresolved questions, not inferred EMV 3DS requirements. [Interpretation; Scheme or Vendor Gap] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 1.5, pp. 18 and 23; section 3.4, pp. 71-72]

## 3. Prerequisites and common AReq/ARes path (Steps 1-6)

### 3.1 Prerequisites

- The 3DS Requestor must gather the information needed for the AReq assembled by the 3DS Server. Section 3.4 lists account information, Merchant Risk Indicator, prior-transaction authentication information, payment or non-payment information, and Cardholder information as possible inputs, without requiring a particular internal component to gather them. [Normative Requirement plus Interpretation; Shall / Descriptive] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, pp. 71-72, Reqs 271 and 273]
- If the Requestor and 3DS Server are separate components, their transferred data must be protected to Payment System security requirements with mutual authentication of both servers. Failure to establish that communication, or failure to deliver the inputs within a reasonable time, ends 3-D Secure processing and in the latter case must be reported to the Requestor. [Normative Requirement; Shall] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, p. 72, Req 271]
- The 3DS Server must obtain the Requestor ID and Server Reference Number, conditionally obtain the Acquirer BIN, generate the 3DS Server Transaction ID, have the Table B.1 inputs, select the DS, and establish its secure DS link before sending AReq. [Normative Requirement; Shall] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, p. 72, Reqs 272-277 and 302]
- The 3DS Server can use cached PRes version ranges to verify protocol support and the ACS Information Indicator to identify account-range features such as Decoupled Authentication. This is an optional capability check, not proof that the ACS will select a Decoupled outcome for the transaction. [Interpretation; Note / can] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, pp. 72-73, Req 277 and following note]
- When assessing disposition, the ACS should use the 3RI Indicator, 3DS Requestor Decoupled Request Indicator, and Requestor Prior Transaction Authentication Information. If it selects Decoupled Authentication, it must also determine whether it supports an acceptable method, based in part on 3DS Requestor Decoupled Max Time. [Normative Requirement; Should / Shall] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, pp. 74-75, Reqs 290-291 and 328]

## 5. Decoupled continuation (Steps 7-13)

### 5.1 State, timing, and indicators

When the ACS decides Decoupled Authentication is necessary, it must set ARes Transaction Status `D`, set ACS Decoupled Confirmation Indicator `Y`, and store the 3DS Server Transaction ID and DS Transaction ID for the later RReq. The ACS then starts a timer against 3DS Requestor Decoupled Max Time and authenticates the Cardholder, with the objective of completing before that maximum expires. How the authentication decision is made is outside scope. [Normative Requirement plus Scheme or Vendor Gap; Shall / explicitly out of scope] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, pp. 75-76, Reqs 328 and 330]

Figure 3.4 shows dashed request/response arrows between the ACS and an Authentication Service and states that dashed arrows are outside the 3-D Secure protocol. It does not name those arrows as EMV 3DS message types, define an issuer API, or show a 3DS Client. The safe implementation conclusion is that the ACS must integrate some issuer authentication capability, but its protocol, UX, and exact Cardholder interaction are outside Section 3.4. [Interpretation plus Issuer ACS Implementation Consideration; Descriptive / out of scope] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, pp. 71 and 76, Figure 3.4, Req 330]

The ACS must send exactly one RReq for every ARes with status `D`. It sends RReq immediately after obtaining a successful or unsuccessful authentication result. If no result exists when Decoupled Max Time expires, it sends an RReq without an authentication result, with a stated one-hour grace period. The 3DS Server must wait at least Decoupled Max Time plus 30 seconds; if it never receives RReq, subsequent processing is outside EMV 3DS and it should assume the Decoupled Authentication was unsuccessful. [Normative Requirement; Shall / Should] [EMV 3DS v2.2.0, section 3.4, pp. 76-77, Reqs 349-354]
